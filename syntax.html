Syntax

My research in syntax focuses on cross-clausal movement dependencies, particularly hyper-raising and superraising, as well as subjecthood and ellipsis, drawing on generative theory and cross-linguistic comparison.

Dissertation

Edge Subjects

PhD dissertation in progress · University of Connecticut

My dissertation, Edge Subjects, develops a cross-linguistic account of hyper-raising and its relation to superraising. Rather than attributing hyper-raising to exceptional properties of the embedded tense or complementizer, I argue that its availability follows from independently observable differences in the structural position of regular subjects.

On this account, the relevant subjects occupy a high position with both A- and A′-properties, which provides a launch site for movement across a finite-clause boundary and avoids an improper-movement derivation. The analysis explains the mixed A/A′ behavior and scope rigidity of subjects in Brazilian Portuguese and derives a broader typological generalization: languages may permit superraising only if they also permit hyper-raising.

My dissertation further tests this account through original fieldwork on Chichewa, where hyper-raising is possible but constrained by animacy.
