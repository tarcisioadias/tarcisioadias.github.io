Syntax

My research in syntax focuses on cross-clausal movement dependencies, particularly hyper-raising and superraising, as well as subjecthood and ellipsis, drawing on generative theory and cross-linguistic comparison.

Dissertation 🪴

Edge Subjects

PhD dissertation in progress · University of Connecticut

My dissertation, Edge Subjects, develops a cross-linguistic account of hyper-raising, a construction in which a subject moves out of a finite clause. Rather than attributing hyper-raising to exceptional properties of tense or complementizers, I argue that its availability follows from independently observable variation in the position of regular subjects. In hyper-raising languages, subjects occupy a higher position with mixed A and A′-properties. This position provides a launch site for movement across the clause boundary without improper movement and predicts rigid subject-over-object scope. This prediction is borne out across the hyper-raising languages surveyed, which do not permit the relevant inverse scope reading. Extending the analysis to superraising, I derive the generalization that only hyper-raising languages may permit superraising and explain why the latter is considerably rarer.

Brazilian Portuguese provides the core evidence for the analysis. My field work on Chichewa uncovers what is, to my knowledge, the first documented animacy restriction on hyper-raising: only inanimate DPs may undergo it, while animate DPs in superficially similar constructions are matrix perceptual sources rather than raised subjects. Related work on Brazilian Portuguese Quem nunca? ellipsis distinguishes locally moved wh-subjects from objects, adjuncts, and long-distance subjects, supporting a separate lower wh-position specifically targeted by local subject movement.
